For Those Who Wait

For the one in a long season that has not resolved — and is not over.

All Scripture is from the World English Bible (WEB), which is in the public domain.

Maybe you have waited months. Maybe years. You have prayed the same prayer so many times that the words feel worn smooth, and well-meaning people have told you to just be patient, as if patience were a switch you had forgotten to flip.

You are waiting on God — for healing, for a loved one, for an answer, for a door that will not open. This page will not explain the delay, and it will not hand you a date. It only wants to sit with you in it for a while, with the words God gave to other people who waited — because Scripture does not treat waiting as a small thing, or as a sign that something is wrong with you. Again and again, it treats waiting as a place where God meets His people.

· ✦ ·

Wait, and be strong

David, at the end of a psalm full of trouble

Wait for Yahweh. Be strong, and let your heart take courage. Yes, wait for Yahweh.

— Psalm 27:14

Notice that waiting, here, is not passive. It takes strength. It takes courage. David is not telling his heart to relax — he is telling it to hold on. If waiting feels like hard work, that is because it is. You are not doing it wrong when it costs you something. The command is not “feel better about this.” The command is: stay. Keep your heart turned toward Him, and let Him be the One you are waiting for — not only the One you are waiting on.

Wings like eagles

To a people who said, “My way is hidden from Yahweh”

He gives power to the weak. He increases the strength of him who has no might. Even the youths faint and get weary, and the young men utterly fall; but those who wait for Yahweh will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.

— Isaiah 40:29–31

God does not begin by scolding the weary. He begins by giving — power to the weak, strength to the one who has none left. Even the young and strong run out; weariness is not a failure of faith, it is the human condition this promise was made for. And look at where the promise lands: not only soaring and running, but walking, and not fainting. The ordinary day. The next small step. God counts that as the miracle too.

Quietly wait

Written from the ruins of a beloved city

Yahweh is good to those who wait for him, to the soul who seeks him. It is good that a man should hope and quietly wait for the salvation of Yahweh.

— Lamentations 3:25–26

These words were not written from a place of comfort. Jeremiah wrote them sitting in the ashes of Jerusalem — the city he loved destroyed, everything he had prayed against come to pass. And it is there, of all places, that he says it: Yahweh is good to those who wait for Him. He did not wait until the rescue came to say it. If your waiting is happening in the middle of loss rather than after it, you are in the company of the man who wrote this. Waiting was honored even there.

More than watchmen for the morning

A psalm sung out of the depths

I wait for Yahweh. My soul waits. I hope in his word. My soul longs for the Lord more than watchmen long for the morning; more than watchmen for the morning.

— Psalm 130:5–6

A watchman on the wall does not long for the morning because he doubts it is coming. He longs for it because the night is long and cold and he is tired, and morning is the surest thing he knows. Your longing is not weak faith. Longing is what certainty feels like at 3 a.m. The psalmist says it twice — more than watchmen for the morning; more than watchmen for the morning — as if once was not enough to hold the ache of it.

Though it delay, wait for it

God’s own answer to a prophet who asked, “How long?”

For the vision is yet for the appointed time, and it hurries toward the end, and won’t prove false. Though it takes time, wait for it; because it will surely come. It won’t delay.

— Habakkuk 2:3

Habakkuk had cried out about violence and injustice and heard, for a while, only silence. When God answered, He did not pretend the wait would be short — He said plainly that it would take time. But He also said the delay is not a denial. There is an appointed time, and it is His, and what He has purposed will not prove false. You may not be able to see it hurrying. From where you stand it may look perfectly still. Stillness is not the same as absence.

My God will hear me

Micah, when even home was not safe

But as for me, I will look to Yahweh. I will wait for the God of my salvation. My God will hear me.

— Micah 7:7

Micah looked around and saw corruption everywhere — courts bought, neighbors untrustworthy, even family turned against family. He had no power to fix any of it. What he had was a direction to look, and a God who hears. “My God will hear me” is not a small thing to be able to say in a long wait. The silence you sit in is not an unanswered line. You are heard — every time, including the times you had no words left and only stayed.

You are not forgotten in the waiting

Waiting is not God being absent, and it is not God being displeased with you. The watchman longs for the morning because morning is certain, not because it is in doubt — and the God who has held you through every night of this long season has not lost track of you in it. He hears you. He is good to those who wait for Him. So wait, and be strong, and let your heart take courage. He is worth waiting for.
